## What Elon actually did at our stage (Zip2, 1995–early 1996)

1. **He left the safe path in days and started building.** In summer 1995 he deferred a Stanford physics PhD after less than a week, kept a deferred admission as a hedge, and started the company that became Zip2. Source: https://thepenngazette.com/from-zip-to-x/

2. **He could not afford an apartment and an office, so he rented only the office, slept on a futon, and showered at the YMCA.** He has told this himself: one computer; the site was up during the day and he coded at night, seven days a week. "Work hard every waking hour… if somebody else is working 50 hours and you're working 100, you'll get twice as much done." Sources: 2014 USC commencement, via https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2018/06/19/how-elon-musk-founded-zip2-with-his-brother-kimbal.html ; Stanford eCorner, 8 Oct 2003, https://ecorner.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2003/10/397.pdf ; Walter Isaacson, *Elon Musk*, ch. 10 (office, YMCA, Jack in the Box, sleeping under the desk).

3. **Burn was absurdly tiny; revenue already beat expenses.** They drilled a hole through the floor to the ISP downstairs for about $100/month of internet. "When we talked to VCs, we could say we were profitable." Sources: same eCorner transcript; also https://www.elonmuskbook.org/the-book-of-elon-musk-free-online-version/starting-zip2

4. **He sold himself, to the people who already had customers and money — then to the biggest names.** Early on they talked to small newspapers and media companies and hired three salespeople on contingency from a newspaper ad (six people total). The strategy became: sell software to big newspapers so those papers could build city guides (they already had sales forces). Knight-Ridder, the New York Times, Pulitzer and Hearst signed up. By 1997 Zip2 was in 140 newspapers at license fees of about $1,000–$10,000. *Editor & Publisher* ran "Newspaperdom's New Superhero: Zip2." Navteq licensed its map database for free until Zip2 was profitable. Kimbal was thrown a Yellow Pages book at the Toronto Star ("Do you honestly think you're ever going to replace this?"). Sources: eCorner 2003; Isaacson ch. 10; https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Newspaperdom%27s+new+super+hero%3a+Zip2.-a020237323 ; https://www.elonmuskbook.org/the-book-of-elon-musk-free-online-version/starting-zip2

5. **Venture money came after a working product and after Netscape made the internet obvious — and the VCs later moved him aside as CEO.** Mohr Davidow invested about $3 million for 60% (Elon has also recalled ~$3.5 million) in 1996. Rich Sorkin was made CEO; Elon became CTO. He later said: "I learned that you could not truly be the chief technology or product officer unless you were the CEO." Sources: Book of Elon / eCorner; Penn Gazette; Isaacson ch. 10.

## Later, still all-in (labeled so we do not confuse stages)

6. **He put the chips back on the table.** Zip2 was sold to Compaq for $307 million in 1999. Elon has said he put about $20 million of his proceeds into X.com (the company that merged into PayPal). Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2018/06/19/how-elon-musk-founded-zip2-with-his-brother-kimbal.html ; https://www.startuparchive.org/p/elon-musk-tells-the-story-of-his-early-career

7. **When he needed a rocket, he went in person.** In 2001–2002 he flew to Russia to try to buy ICBMs for a Mars mission, was turned down, and decided to build the rockets himself (SpaceX, 2002). After three failed Falcon 1 launches the company was nearly out of money; the fourth succeeded in 2008, in the same stretch Tesla was also near failure. This is after PayPal capital — not our cash position — but the method is the same as Zip2: go there yourself, do not outsource the hard ask, do not stop after "no." Source for the early-career retelling: https://www.startuparchive.org/p/elon-musk-tells-the-story-of-his-early-career ; Isaacson covers Russia / Falcon 1 / 2008 in the SpaceX and Tesla chapters.
